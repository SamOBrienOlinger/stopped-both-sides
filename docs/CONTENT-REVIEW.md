## Evidence and limitations

- INAR’s live site was inspected for visual styling: Open Sans, teal `#54a7a7`, white surfaces, slate `#53606e`, rose accents and rounded cards. A darker teal is used where needed for readable text contrast.
- Citizens Information pages were available through indexed search extracts; direct fetching returned HTTP 403. The product’s source note discloses that access limitation. Specific legal propositions were cross-checked against legislation, the DPP, ICCL’s guide, the Legal Aid Board or Fiosrú.
- ICCL’s 2014 guide was retrieved and read, including searches, arrest, legal assistance and statements. Its date is explicit throughout the source register.
- The INAR/ICCL 2024 report was read as community research, not treated as a current procedural manual. Characters and dialogue are original fiction, not quoted participant accounts.
- Revised sections 23 and 21 of the Misuse of Drugs Act 1977 and section 24 of the Criminal Justice (Public Order) Act 1994 support the specific search, obstruction and identification examples.
- Revised Children Act 2001 sections 60 and 61 support the youth scenarios. Adult presence and independent legal advice are distinct; the statutory exceptions are acknowledged.
- DPP custody guidance supports legal advice, interpretation, medical care and notification. The game does not promise unrestricted personal phone use.
- Fiosrú’s current official FAQ confirms its replacement of GSOC in April 2025, its normal 12-month complaint limit, possible late acceptance for good reason, and the ability to complain without knowing an officer’s name.
- The game does not give a universal detention limit. General online guides describe different maxima. The correct limit depends on the specific statutory basis, authorised extensions and calculation of detention time.
- The game does not promise universal free representation. Users are directed to ask about the applicable legal-aid scheme and eligibility.

## Editorial boundaries

No automatic link between identity, choice and arrest. No claim that a particular response prevents racism. No assessment of a real person’s legal case. No risk or guilt scoring. No real incident information is collected. No complaint is submitted on a player’s behalf.

A response marked for review indicates a learning misconception, not blame for an adverse encounter. Feedback distinguishes legal rules, practical prompts and fictional next scenes. All source IDs are attached to the relevant decision node and remain available in the recap.

## Verification performed

JavaScript syntax and local entrypoint/import/font/icon checks; complete traversal of all 594 possible answer sequences; no unreachable nodes; branch changes discard abandoned choices; replay creates a fresh session; repeated or invalid choices cannot advance twice. These tests cover game logic and source references, not the legal correctness of the cited propositions.

Browser, device, screen-reader and moderated community testing have not been performed. Responsive CSS includes layouts for narrow screens and reduced-motion settings, but those provisions are not a claim of real-device certification.
